S12.5. Audit-service interpretation and negative stopping

An audit-service interpretation. Consider indivisible service configurations with frozen benchmark archives, known lengths and a contract that charges for each next whole record. The two mean losses describe each configuration; the disclosure fee measures the separate cost of obtaining evidence. A nondominated configuration may meet separate eligibility constraints while every nonzero nonnegative weighted sum over the full catalogue omits it: among (0,1)(0,1), (1,0)(1,0) and (3/5,3/5)(3/5,3/5), only the last meets both bounds 7/107/10. The audit concerns omissions of scalarisation on the full catalogue. Filtering by already known eligibility constraints first defines a different selection problem and leaves only the eligible point in this example. Admitting mixtures would change the decision, since the equal mixture of the first two dominates it. This proposed contract requires fixed order, value-independent prices and the stated legal completions. Its verdict concerns archived means. Sections S5.4, S10 and S12 give the formal extensions, a local demonstrator and the proposed service contract. A strict-margin theorem additionally guarantees avoided disclosures against census, without giving the policy those retrospective margins as input (Section S5.7). For example, complete triangle means (7/8,3/8)(7/8,3/8), (1/2,1/2)(1/2,1/2) and (1,0)(1,0) guarantee that lockstep acquisition saves at least 27 of 480 disclosures when N=160N=160, whatever their legal row order. This is an analytic guarantee for specified means, not measured economic benefit. Monetary, transmission or energy savings require an operational cost model.

Negative stopping. A wrapper can stop a valid policy early when an observed prefix forces FALSE. It suffices that a rival upper corner dominates the candidate lower corner, or that some nonzero nonnegative weight supports the candidate upper corner against every rival lower corner. These sufficient tests preserve every positive paid-query transcript and weakly reduce negative acquisition cost on every fixed archive and random seed. They need valid coordinate bounds, rather than attainable endpoints or independent completions. They are not a complete FALSE oracle; their extra computation is excluded from the disclosure-price theorem. Section S11.2 proves the wrapper statement, and Sections S11.3–S11.4 report its archived-data replays.
