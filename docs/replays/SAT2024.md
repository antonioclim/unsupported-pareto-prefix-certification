### S11.4. SAT Competition 2024: a retained negative-only replay
The official SAT Competition 2024 main-track detailed results provide a second archived-data replay [[39](../REFERENCES.md#ref-39)]. The declared protocol retains all 14 solver columns and all 400 rows in the supplied instance-hash order. Every unordered solver triple and every designated candidate is used, giving $\binom{14}{3}\cdot3=1,092$ tasks, without filtering by the resulting verdict. The 5,600 score cells include 1,827 exact 10,000 penalties for unsolved instances; every other recorded score was checked to lie in $[0,5000]$. The published aggregate score and solved-count table agrees at its displayed precision with the reconstructed values.

For each recorded score $s$, define

$$
(c,f)=\left(\frac{s}{10000},\,\mathbf 1\{s=10000\}\right).
$$

These are normalised PAR-2 penalty and failure objectives, not elapsed CPU time and energy. They satisfy $0\leq f\leq c\leq1$. The closed independent triangle is declared as a conservative audit completion contract; it is not asserted to be the exact physical image of benchmark outcomes. Public aggregates and alternative public access are intentionally withheld from the simulated client. A certificate guarantee under that declared contract does not identify the cheapest certificate under a tighter physical domain.

The implemented candidate-census mixture is the retained policy of main Theorem 19, with its exact rational $p(G)$, rather than the new $87/80$ policy. Its northwest-first and southeast-first branches are both executed, with and without the same FALSE wrapper. The candidate gap is available before a branch-dependent decision: whenever the wrapped branch traces diverge, the replay checks that the candidate has been completed. Thus no hidden outcome selects the schedule.

None of the 1,092 tasks is GOOD; no candidate is GOOD in the complete 14-solver catalogue either. This adverse result supplies no empirical validation of positive certificates or their competitive ratios. The wrapped mixture uses approximately $3.64981\%$ fewer disclosures than census with the same wrapper, $22.7304\%$ fewer than full census, and $7.79044\%$ fewer than its own unaugmented version. These are fixed-cohort negative-verdict comparisons, without a cost-optimal negative comparator or a general claim of superiority to every negative-input policy.
