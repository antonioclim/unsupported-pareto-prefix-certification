S1. Square certificate geometry and acquisition

This section supplies the square-domain geometry and acquisition arguments. There are K≥3K\geq3 groups, each containing N≥1N\geq1 ordered rows; the initially unknown candidate is group 00. Queries reveal whole rows in prefix order, including paid candidate rows. Algorithms must be correct on both answers. The completion domain is the independent continuous product [0,B]×[0,1][0,B]\times[0,1], with B>0B>0 public. Dividing the first coordinate by BB preserves nondomination, all nonnegative scalarisations and query costs. We therefore work in the unit square. No relation between KK and NN is required.

Each disclosure costs one. GOOD means that the complete candidate mean is Pareto-nondominated and unsupported by every nonzero nonnegative scalarisation, with both coordinates minimised. Pareto domination requires at least one strict coordinate improvement; scalarisation ties and the coordinate axes count as support. Admissible policies are causal, zero-error and almost surely terminating on every legal positive and negative input. Let CB*(I)C_B^*(I) be the least total prefix length forcing TRUE over every compatible square completion of the fixed positive archive II. Let CU*(I)C_U^*(I) be its corresponding comparator when the first row coordinate instead has the unbounded domain [0,∞)[0,\infty). Neither retrospective comparator is supplied to a policy. Define

RB*(K,N)=inf𝒜supI:GOOD(I)𝔼C𝒜(I)CB*(I), R_B^*(K,N)=\inf_{\mathcal A}\sup_{I:\mathrm{GOOD}(I)} \frac{\mathbb E C_{\mathcal A}(I)}{C_B^*(I)},

where each expectation is taken on a fixed archive before the input supremum. Write Rdet,B*R_{\mathrm{det},B}^* for the same optimum over deterministic policies. Throughout, D=K+3N−3D=K+3N-3 and

δK={(4K−13)/6,K even,(4K−13)/6+1/[3(K−1)],K odd. \delta_K= \begin{cases} (4K-13)/6,&K\text{ even},\\ (4K-13)/6+1/[3(K-1)],&K\text{ odd}. \end{cases}

These are the constants from the general catalogue theorem in main Section 3.

S1.1. Exact corners and a faster retrospective comparator

At a prefix of length srs_r, let SrS_r be the observed coordinate sum and set

Every compatible mean belongs to the rectangle [Lr,Ur][L_r,U_r], and each point of that rectangle is attainable. For a missing-row count q>0q>0, assign all missing rows the same pair N(p−Lr)/qN(p-L_r)/q. Its coordinates lie in [0,1][0,1]. For the first condition, a dominating corner pair itself gives a legal counterexample. Conversely, any domination in a completion implies domination of the corresponding corner pair: Lr≤pr≤p0≤U0L_r\leq p_r\leq p_0\leq U_0, with a strict inequality surviving in some coordinate. These statements also explain why full-vector equality is safe for nondomination, whereas a scalarisation tie remains support.

without imposing a restricted certificate syntax. All prefixes and minima here refer to the realised archive II.
