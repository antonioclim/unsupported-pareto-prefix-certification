S11.2. Sound negative stopping and preservation of positive guarantees

Lemma S15 (sufficient FALSE certificates and a stopping wrapper). Suppose the completed mean of every configuration gg lies coordinatewise between known bounds LgL_g and UgU_g. Either of the following conditions certifies FALSE:

  1. Some rival gg has Ug≤LAU_g\leq L_A coordinatewise, with at least one strict coordinate.
  2. There is a nonzero nonnegative vector ww such that w⋅UA≤w⋅Lgw\cdot U_A\leq w\cdot L_g for every rival gg.

The bounds need only be valid; they need not be attainable, and no independence of the legal completions is required for these sufficient tests. Let a correct, terminating acquisition policy be augmented by checking these tests after every purchased record and returning FALSE as soon as either succeeds. For every fixed positive archive, its paid path is unchanged for each random seed on which the original policy is correct. On every negative archive the augmented paid path is a prefix of the original one. Consequently it preserves the original positive-input competitive guarantee and weakly decreases acquisition charges pathwise under nonnegative additive query charges and an identical compulsory setup charge.

Proof. In the first case every legal completion satisfies pg≤Ug≤LA≤pAp_g\leq U_g\leq L_A\leq p_A, with a strict inequality in the indicated coordinate, so the candidate is dominated. In the second case every legal completion satisfies

w⋅pA≤w⋅UA≤w⋅Lg≤w⋅pg(g≠A). w\cdot p_A\leq w\cdot U_A\leq w\cdot L_g\leq w\cdot p_g \qquad(g\ne A).

The same permitted nonzero nonnegative weight supports the candidate in every completion, with ties allowed. In either case GOOD is false. Couple the original and augmented policies using the same private random seed. A sound FALSE test cannot succeed on a positive archive, so its execution is unchanged. On a negative archive the augmented policy follows the original until either it stops earlier with a correct FALSE certificate or the original terminates; it therefore terminates and purchases a prefix of the original paid path. Summing nonnegative charges proves the accounting claim. Internal computation is excluded from this charge comparison. ▫\square

For rational bounds in two objectives, write w=(a,1−a)w=(a,1-a) with 0≤a≤10\leq a\leq1. Each rival’s inequality in the second test is a closed linear interval in aa; intersect these intervals exactly and export a rational point whenever the intersection is nonempty. Coordinate-axis weights and equality are permitted. The two tests are elementary sufficient conditions, without a claim of a complete FALSE oracle or historical novelty. Their finite soundness and incompleteness control is reported in Section S10. Valid conservative bounds can also certify FALSE for a tighter physical domain, but this observation does not identify that domain’s cheapest TRUE certificate or transfer a positive competitive equality.

S12. Designed audit services and application scenarios

S12.1. A designed service for auditing indivisible alternatives

Consider an audit of a catalogue of indivisible service configurations. Each configuration has an immutable, known-length benchmark archive, with a publicly fixed record order. A record contains two exact losses, for example an operating-cost measure and a service penalty. The mean losses describe the archived configurations; a disclosure fee measures the separate cost of obtaining evidence. The audit asks whether a designated nondominated configuration is omitted by every nonzero nonnegative weighted score. Its answer concerns the archived means, without estimating future population performance or selecting a utility-maximising configuration.

Such an audit can identify an option that matters under separate eligibility constraints. With candidate p=(3/5,3/5)p=(3/5,3/5) and rivals L=(0,1)L=(0,1) and R=(1,0)R=(1,0), only pp meets both coordinate bounds 7/107/10. It is nondominated, yet for every nonzero nonnegative weight the average rival score is strictly smaller than its score, so one rival beats it. A search confined to weighted-sum minimisers therefore misses the only eligible configuration in this catalogue. The object of the audit is this omission from the full catalogue. If eligibility has already been established and used to filter the catalogue, only pp remains and scalarisation no longer omits it; that is a different selection procedure. If implementations can mix configurations and their losses average linearly, the equal mixture (L+R)/2=(1/2,1/2)(L+R)/2=(1/2,1/2) dominates pp. Indivisibility is thus a substantive application assumption. Randomising the order of inspection does not require or justify randomising the deployed configuration.

The disclosure contract must specify what can be purchased. Each accepted request returns the next whole record of the chosen configuration, including paid candidate records, at a public price fixed independently of the hidden value. Known lengths, immutable records and the admissible completion class are public. Informative side metadata, requests that skip predecessors and free access to the underlying archive would change the observation model. SensorThings offers useful retrieval primitives, including ordering, pagination and filtering (OGC21); its $skip and $filter options do not enforce compulsory paid prefixes. A gateway implementing this contract would therefore require additional access controls. The public APEX archive and the local demonstrators of Section S10 do not establish that such a gateway exists or that its controls have been secured.

Under exact transcript, domain and charge equivalence, the model’s results apply to this designed service. A lower bound or equality of minimax values also requires the service’s admissible inputs to include the corresponding hard archives; an upper guarantee on a narrower class need not establish that converse. When the service has a tighter completion class, the comparator-distortion condition in Section S5.5 quantifies the additional requirement. This gives a testable route from an implemented contract to an acquisition guarantee without inferring a monetary or energy saving from a disclosure count.

The economic objective differs from sequential search for a valuable option. Weitzman’s reservation-rule model uses known reward distributions and investigation costs to optimise expected reward net of investigation costs (W79). Here a fixed archive has a Boolean audit verdict, correctness is required on every admissible archive and the comparator is its cheapest realised certificate. Adding a prior and a monetary utility would define a further decision problem; it would not turn the present certificate ratio into expected economic welfare.

S12.3. Conditions for an operational benefit claim

For each scenario, the implementation should document the catalogue, the designated candidate, both objective definitions, the archive lengths and order, the information available before acquisition and the legal completions of every unread prefix. Numerical encoding and endpoint attainability require explicit treatment. Independence here concerns the Cartesian-product structure of allowed completions where the applicable theorem requires it; independent sampling is not a substitute. Public dependencies should be modelled directly or accompanied by a proved comparator-distortion bound. The transfer arguments in Part I then determine which certificate and acquisition guarantees survive the interpretation.

The numerical triangle bound applies to its stated three-configuration domain and price assumptions. Merely assigning two losses to records does not establish those assumptions, and the same constant has not been proved for arbitrary catalogue sizes or tariffs. The contract must also charge candidate observations and prevent access paths that bypass compulsory predecessors. Supporting weights and random inspection choices concern the audit; they do not authorise mixing the deployed alternatives.

A practical evaluation should report evidence fees, setup, computation, storage and service overhead separately, then compare total costs against a declared operational baseline on the same task. It should retain adverse cases and distinguish positive-certificate cost from the cost of handling negative inputs. A smaller competitive ratio does not, by itself, demonstrate a positive monetary benefit against another audit method.
