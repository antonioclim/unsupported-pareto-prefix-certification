P22 — Exact triangular five-row lower witness
Date: 23 October 2026
Status: analytic lower proof plus exact rational enumeration. Historical priority
not assessed here. No global upper is proved in this note.

Contract
Three configurations A (candidate), L (northwest rival), R (southeast rival)
have N=5 ordered whole rows. Independent unread rows range over the triangle
0<=f<=c<=1. Both coordinates are minimised. GOOD means nondomination and
unsupportedness by every nonzero nonnegative linear scalarisation; axes and
ties count as support. Algorithms terminate and are zero-error on all legal
inputs. Positive inputs are scored against their cheapest realised TRUE
prefix certificate. No promise of membership in these two worlds is supplied.

1. Fixed archives
The following observations are common to both worlds:
- Candidate rows 1–4: (79/80,1/8), four times.
- L row 1: (51/100,51/100).
- L rows 2–4: (13/50,13/50), three times.
- All five R rows: (1,0).
The differing final rows are:
World A: candidate row 5=(0,0), L row 5=(13/50,13/50).
World B: candidate row 5=(1,0), L row 5=(1,1).
Both archives are fixed before algorithmic private randomness.

The candidate lower corner after four rows is p=(79/100,1/10), and its
upper corner is p+(1/5,1/5)=(99/100,3/10).
The complete L point in A is (31/100,31/100).
L's upper corner after four rows, in either world, is (229/500,229/500).
This is also its complete point in B.
The complete candidate point in B is (99/100,1/10).
L's upper corner after one row is (451/500,451/500).

2. Exact TRUE-prefix sets
The P21 triangular feasibility theorem implies every TRUE prefix has at least
four candidate observations. It therefore suffices to consider a=4 or a=5.

World A:
Every R prefix of length at most four has upper failure at least 1/5, whereas
all L upper failures are at least 31/100. Candidate lower failure is 1/10
at a=4 and a=5. Thus the failure axis supports the candidate lower corner
until R is complete: P4 cannot be robust before j=5.
Every L prefix of length at most four has upper coordinate sum at least
2(229/500)=229/250, whereas the candidate lower sum is 89/100 and the
complete R sum is 1. Equal weights therefore support the candidate lower
corner until L is complete. Hence i=5 is necessary.
At (a,i,j)=(4,5,5), robust P2 holds strictly: L failure 31/100 exceeds
candidate upper failure 3/10, and R cost 1 exceeds candidate upper cost
99/100. With weight 5/16 on L, the rival convex combination is
(251/320,31/320), strictly coordinatewise below (79/100,1/10).
Thus (4,5,5) is TRUE, and monotonicity also makes (5,5,5) TRUE.
The complete TRUE-prefix set is exactly {(4,5,5),(5,5,5)}.
Its unit-price minimum cost is 14.

World B:
At candidate prefix four, even full rival observations give points
(229/500,229/500) and (1,0). Equal weights support the lower candidate
(79/100,1/10), because 229/250>89/100 and 1>89/100.
Thus a=5 is necessary. If R has at most four observations, both rival upper
failures exceed candidate failure 1/10, so the failure axis supports it.
Thus j=5 is necessary. With L unobserved, its lower corner (0,0) dominates
the complete candidate, so i>=1 is necessary.
At (5,1,5), P2 holds: L lower failure 51/500 exceeds candidate failure
1/10, and R cost 1 exceeds candidate cost 99/100. Put weight 21/200 on
L's upper corner (451/500,451/500). The combination with R=(1,0) is
(98971/100000,9471/100000), strictly below (99/100,1/10).
Hence (5,1,5) is TRUE. By monotonicity, precisely the states
{(5,i,5):1<=i<=5} are TRUE. The unit-price minimum cost is 11.

3. Arbitrary adaptive randomised algorithms
The worlds agree until either candidate row 5 or L row 5 is acquired.
No TRUE prefix precedes both distinguishing observations. Each algorithm
correct on the whole legal class must acquire at least one of them almost
surely on these positive worlds. Let q be the probability that candidate
row 5 is acquired first. The same q applies on both fixed archives, since
all prior histories, including arbitrary prefix interleavings and private
randomness, have identical distributions.
In A, every TRUE certificate already costs 14 and excludes candidate row 5
only at its unique minimum. Acquiring that row first incurs a further cost
one. Thus E[C(A)]>=14+q.
In B, every TRUE certificate requires the candidate and all R rows plus
at least L row 1. If L row 5 is acquired before candidate row 5, the prefix
constraint forces all five L rows; the total cost is at least 15 rather
than the minimum 11. Thus E[C(B)]>=15-4q.
Consequently the worst-case ratio is at least
 max{(14+q)/14,(15-4q)/11}.
The minimum occurs at q=56/67 and equals 71/67.
For a deterministic algorithm, q is zero or one; its lower is min(15/11,
15/14)=15/14.
These are lower bounds for the whole continuous triangular problem, not
merely promised-family competitive values. A matching global upper must
be supplied separately.

4. Arbitrary fixed positive row prices (lower only)
Allow any public fixed strictly positive row prices, independent of hidden
values, and common compulsory information-free setup F>=0.
Let W be F plus prices of candidate rows 1–4, L row 1 and all five R rows.
Let x>0 be the price of candidate row 5, and y>0 the sum of L row prices 2–5.
The exact comparator costs of A and B are W+y and W+x respectively.
The same first-distinguishing-tail argument gives expected costs at least
 W+y+q*x and W+x+(1-q)*y.
Balancing yields
 q=y(W+y)/[x(W+x)+y(W+y)],
 R_randomised >= 1+xy/[x(W+x)+y(W+y)],
 R_deterministic >= 1+min(x,y)/[W+max(x,y)].
Intermediate L-tail observations may add costs; they cannot invalidate
these lower bounds. At unit prices W=10,x=1,y=4, recovering 71/67 and15/14.
For a common setup F, the unit-row lower bounds become
 1+4/(5F+67) and 1+1/(F+14).
No weighted global upper is asserted by this note.

5. Exact finite-alphabet witness
All actual observations belong to the fixed six-symbol alphabet
 {(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(79/80,1/8),(51/100,51/100),(13/50,13/50)}.
Both complete-vector endpoints are included. Independent completion from
this known alphabet therefore preserves every robust TRUE state on these
archives, by the endpoint-domain identity in the manuscript. The same
lower values hold on this declared finite alphabet, its uniform 1/400
triangular grid, and any triangular superset containing the six symbols.
If the continuous-triangle upper71/67 and15/14 is independently proved,
these lower witnesses and the same complete policies establish global
exact equality on the six-symbol alphabet as well. No minimum-alphabet
claim is made.

check_triangle_n5_pair.py enumerated all216prefix triples in each fixed
world (432states), using Fraction arithmetic and the exact support-interval
