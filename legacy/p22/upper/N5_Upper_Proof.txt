EXACT FIVE-ROW TRIANGULAR ACQUISITION: GLOBAL UPPER BOUNDS
Working proof, 23 October 2026. This is a new internal research note, not a
claim of historical priority, external peer review or formal verification.

Model. There are three labelled configurations, designated candidate A and
two rivals, each with N=5 ordered whole rows in T={(c,f):0<=f<=c<=1}.
Unseen rows are independently completable. A query reveals the next row of
one configuration and costs one. GOOD means Pareto nondomination and absence
of any nonzero nonnegative supporting linear scalarisation; equality is
support. Algorithms must terminate correctly on every legal input. Their
positive-input cost is divided by the minimum realised TRUE-prefix cost.
A fixed compulsory information-free setup F>=0 is added to both costs.

The exact prefix-corner test and oriented triangular feasibility criterion
are inherited from P21. No assertion below concerns noisy measurements or
a more restrictive physical completion domain.

1. FEASIBLE PREFIX SHAPES

For candidate length a, northwest rival length i and southeast rival length
j, write Z=i+2j+2a-20. A TRUE state of these lengths exists precisely when
Z>0 and Z^2>4(10-a-i)(10-a-j). Among the 125 triples in {1,...,5}^3,
the componentwise minimal feasible triples are exactly

    (4,5,5), (5,1,5), (5,5,3).

This finite algebraic reduction can also be checked as follows. The P21
candidate bound gives a>=4. With a=4 the criterion forces i=j=5.
With a=5, i,j<=4 gives its largest value at i=j=4, where Z^2=4 and
4(10-a-i)(10-a-j)=4, so strict feasibility fails. Thus a rival is full.
For j=5 every i>=1 is feasible. For i=5 feasibility reduces to j>5/2,
so j>=3. These statements give the three minimal shapes without relying
on the accompanying enumeration.

Consequences: each TRUE prefix costs at least 11; a certificate that does
not census the candidate must be precisely (4,5,5), costing 14. Every
candidate-census certificate has a full rival. If its cost is 11 or 12,
its northwest prefix has respectively one or two rows and its southeast
rival is full. Costs 13 and 14 are treated separately below.

2. GEOMETRIC SCREENING WITH A COMPLETE CANDIDATE

After candidate census, let p=(p_c,p_f) and g=p_c-p_f. At any positive
certificate the rival upper corners strictly cross p. The northwest one
has cost below p_c and failure above p_f; the southeast one has cost above
p_c and failure below p_f. The first P2-safe rival prefix is the first
prefix whose lower corner does not dominate p. On a positive archive,
every such screening query is compulsory in every candidate-census TRUE
certificate. Its side is observable: the northwest lower failure exceeds
p_f and the southeast lower cost exceeds p_c.

If a safe corner is on neither required strict crossing side, or the two
rivals are on the same side, the archive cannot be positive. The policy
may use full census to decide all such cases. This is an all-input policy,
not one promised a positive answer.

The elementary P21 inequalities give, for a partial northwest prefix i,

    g > 1-i/5,

and for a partial southeast prefix j,

    g < 2j/5-1.

Hence:

* if g<=1/5, every candidate-census certificate censuses the northwest;
* if g>=3/5, every candidate-census certificate censuses the southeast;
* if 1/5<g<=2/5, both rival prefixes are at least four;
* if 2/5<g<3/5, northwest prefix is at least three and southeast at least
  four.

In an exterior branch, screen to the first safe prefixes, identify the
two sides, complete the compulsory side, then extend the other side only
until TRUE or census. This obtains the componentwise minimum candidate-
census certificate, and is therefore exactly optimal within that class.

3. POLICY A: CANDIDATE CENSUS

First census A. Always use the exact robust TRUE test before taking a new
row when a TRUE stop is possible, and use full census to decide any
remaining negative archive.

Exterior branches g<=1/5 and g>=3/5 use the exact policy just described.

For 1/5<g<=2/5, acquire the first four rows of each labelled rival
directly. The base (5,4,4), cost13, is compulsory and cannot itself be
TRUE. Reveal the two remaining rows in a fair random order, stopping if
the first completes a TRUE certificate. Every positive archive admitting
a cost14 candidate-census certificate then has expected cost at most14.5.

For 2/5<g<3/5, screen to the first safe prefixes, identify northwest and
southeast, and raise them, if needed, to lengths three and four. All these
rows are compulsory in every candidate-census certificate. If screening
already censused a rival, that census is also compulsory, so extending the
other rival only until TRUE gives the componentwise minimum certificate.
Otherwise the northwest length is three or four and southeast length four.
Independently choose:

    probability 1/4: complete northwest first, test TRUE, then southeast;
    probability 3/4: complete southeast first, then extend northwest only
                     until TRUE.

There is no possible TRUE state with both rivals partial, so completing
the first selected side cannot skip a feasible early TRUE stop. For a
cost13 candidate-census certificate, the only possibility in this branch
is (5,3,5). Southeast-first costs13; northwest-first costs at most15.
Thus its expected cost is at most13.5. For a cost14 certificate of type
(5,4,5), the two respective bounds are14 and15, giving at most14.25.
For type (5,5,4), they are15 and14, giving at most14.75. If more than one
type is available these bounds only improve. Mandatory screening cases
already handled have exact cost and therefore obey the same bounds.

Policy A consequently satisfies the following bounds, where C* denotes
row cost, excluding setup:

    C*=11: A=11.
    C*=12: A=12.
    C*=13: E[A]<=27/2.
    C*=14, no candidate-partial certificate: E[A]<=59/4.
    C*=14, a candidate-partial certificate exists: A<=15.
    C*=15: A=15.

For the first two classes, the northwest prefix i<=2 requires g>3/5,
so the exact exterior branch applies. For C*=13 the only non-exterior
case is (5,3,5) in the last branch. All setup costs are paid once.

4. POLICY B AND THE MIXTURE

Policy B acquires four candidate rows and both rivals completely. Test
TRUE at cost14; if false, reveal the last candidate row and decide by
full census. Thus B costs14 whenever the candidate-partial certificate
exists and otherwise15. The order within its prescribed base is immaterial.

Let d=5F+67. Select Policy A before observations with probability

    p=4(F+14)/d,

and Policy B with probability 1-p=(F+11)/d. Since F>=0 these are legal.
The target competitive ratio is

    R=1+4/d.

For C*=11, mixture overhead is 4(1-p)=4(F+11)/d, exactly R-1 times
the denominator F+11. For a C*=14 archive admitting the candidate-
partial certificate, overhead is at most p=4(F+14)/d, again equality.
For the other classes the overhead numerators, after multiplying by d,
are bounded by:

    C*=12: 3(F+11) <= 4(F+12);
    C*=13: d[2-(3/2)p] = 4F+50 < 4(F+13);
    C*=14 without partial: d[1-p/4] = 4F+53 < 4(F+14);
    C*=15: zero.

These exhaustive classes prove the global upper bound

    R_rand,T^*(3,5;F) <= 1+4/(5F+67).

5. DETERMINISTIC UPPER

Use Policy A alone. In the middle symmetric branch take either rival tail
first deterministically. In the last branch always complete the southeast
first. Every C*<=13 archive then costs exactly C*: the only uncertain
cost13 type is (5,3,5), acquired first. Every C*=14 archive costs at most15,
and C*=15 costs15. Therefore

    R_det,T^*(3,5;F) <= 1+1/(F+14).

The independent fixed-world construction supplied by the parallel lower-
bound task matches both upper bounds. At F=0 the resulting values are
71/67 and15/14. The present file establishes the global upper policies;
it does not rely on the lower worlds for policy correctness.

6. VERIFICATION SCOPE

The proof covers the continuous triangular domain and all F>=0. The own
stdlib checker verifies all125 length triples, the exhaustive certificate
cost partition, the elementary policy cost envelopes and the exact rational
mixture arithmetic for representative F. It is a finite arithmetic check,
not exhaustive sampling of all numerical archives and not formal proof.
The source and retained JSON report must accompany this proof.
