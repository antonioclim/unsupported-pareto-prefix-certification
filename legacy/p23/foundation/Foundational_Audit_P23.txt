1. THE ORDINAL POSTERIOR IS VALID

An adaptive policy may use the relative order of all queried values, including
orders within a side. Under the uniform rank-to-label assignment, a specified
within-L observed ordering has probability 1/a! for every a-subset of L ranks;
the analogous factor is 1/b! on R. Conditioning on the complete observed
ordinal history therefore leaves rank subsets uniform subject to not having
both good ranks. A policy's action probabilities are constant across worlds
producing that same history, so adaptivity does not introduce a hidden
likelihood distinction within that conditional class.

The conditional success probabilities b/(mn-ab) and a/(mn-ab) are correct.
The displayed Bellman recurrence follows after multiplication by mn-ab.
The balanced path maximises the prefix product at every time, respecting
side capacities, and therefore minimises its entire sum of survival
probabilities. This is an optimality proof for the adaptive ordinal game,
not merely a comparison between preselected schedules.

Independent finite execution: 133 labelled rank worlds; 53,701 compatible
world/history pairs; 27,255 posterior classes; 33,431 next-label probability
checks. Histories include all within-side orders for the eight recorded
splits. The Bellman value matched the formula on all 225 splits with
1<=m,n<=15, using 18,225 states. The balanced split maximised the formula for
all 79 total-label counts from 2 through 80. All checks used Fraction and
integer arithmetic and passed. These finite checks do not prove the
infinite Ramsey step.

2. CAUSAL RANDOMISED KERNELS AND RAMSEY QUANTIFIERS

Private randomness can retain hidden internal state. Conditioning on the
completed labelled history integrates over that state. Because every
uncompleted prefix row in the embedding is fixed independently of hidden
thresholds, the event of reaching a given completed history depends only on
the values in that history, not on unqueried thresholds. Thus the behavioural
kernel is causal. Worlds with identical revealed history induce identical
events in the space of random tapes and identical conditional action laws.

The colouring includes every label sequence and every no-edge value-order
assignment for a fixed subset size. It is finite for each finite k and eta.
Successive homogeneous restrictions for d=1,...,k-1 are legitimate because
restriction of an already homogeneous set preserves its homogeneity.
Using an actual representative probability vector, rather than a rounded
vector, preserves normalisation and forbidden queried-label coordinates.
Per-coordinate errors below eta imply total variation <=k*eta/2. At most k
coupled decisions and a query-count range [0,k] give the stated expectation
error <=k^3*eta/2.

Unreachable histories do not require a physical conditional simulation.
Arbitrary distributions there remain admissible; homogeneity still bounds
their difference to corresponding reachable representatives on a common
coupling path. If the coupling diverges, the bounded-horizon error already
accounts for all subsequent behaviour.

The valid quantifier is: for each policy and each epsilon>0, choose a finite
numeric set and then a fixed input before private randomness is realised.
No one finite prior works uniformly for all numeric policies by this proof.
Letting epsilon decrease is done inside the supremum over inputs; exact
attainment or a uniform bounded bit length does not follow. P21 and P22
state those limitations. No adversary is selected separately for each
random tape.

3. AN EXPLICIT CORRECTION TO POSITIVE CONTINUATIONS

P21 Appendix A.3 says that if one good endpoint is observed its missing
opposite can be inserted "between the observed extremes". That interval is
wrong when one side is exhausted: a missing R needs to lie ABOVE the smallest
observed L, and a missing L BELOW the largest observed R. The ambient dense
rationals permit both repairs. The complete replacement and all cases appear
in P21_Corrected_Continuation_Paragraph.txt. Observed ranks and the selected
integer set S are not modified; legal completions may use values outside S.
The correction makes explicit what the proof needs, rather than weakening
the all-input correctness requirement.

4. FINITE-TRANSCRIPT SOUNDNESS LEMMA

Lemma. Consider a causal randomised evaluator of a Boolean predicate on a
fixed finite table. A query reveals the next unread row of a selected group.
Assume zero-error correctness on every legal fixed input. For each fixed
input I, with probability one every terminating TRUE answer is based on a
transcript that forces TRUE over all legal completions. The analogous claim
holds for FALSE. Exact real-valued rows are allowed.

Proof. For fixed I there are finitely many query-label sequences without
repeated rows, since at most KN rows can be revealed. Hence there are only
finitely many possible observed transcripts h on I. If a noncertifying h
preceded a TRUE declaration with positive probability, select one legal
FALSE completion J agreeing on all revealed rows of h. For each private
random tape producing h and that declaration on I, causality produces the
same queries, revealed values, internal transitions and declaration on J.
The positive-probability error event therefore also occurs on the single
fixed input J, contradicting zero-error correctness. Each noncertifying
transcript has zero such probability; their finite union has probability
zero. The argument also works for countably many finite transcripts if
redundant queries are retained. No union over an uncountable family of
input-dependent random null sets is needed. QED.

This elementary clarification explains why geometric certificate necessity
can be charged almost surely for randomised algorithms. It is not presented
as a historical novelty claim. Usual measurability of the algorithm's
random events is part of speaking of its probabilities and expectations.

5. COMPRESSION AND COMPULSORY PAYMENTS

In the unbounded and bounded hard embeddings, candidate census and one row
from every rival are compulsory on each positive fixed archive. A completed
rival purchases its remaining N-1 distinct rows. Charging H in advance is
therefore only an accounting device: those rows are not supplied free to the
actual algorithm and are disjoint from each charged tail. Interleaving or
prefetch cannot erase these eventual payments. Stopping the compressed
process at the first observed inversion only reduces its completion count.

At no-edge completed histories, the corrected dense rational continuation
excludes an early FALSE declaration. A compatible supported completion
excludes early TRUE. Almost-sure termination on the compatible positive
input forces a next completion. These facts justify the next-label kernel
without assuming that the original policy completes one group at a time.

6. ENDPOINTS AND THE BAND EMBEDDING

The endpoint theorem needs the entire vectors (0,0) and (1,1), legal in each
missing row, and independent choice of missing rows. It does not need the
intervening rectangle to be attainable. The robust-P2 counterexample uses
candidate upper and rival lower endpoints; the supporting-weight
counterexample uses candidate lower and all rival upper endpoints. Both
constructions remain legal for the bands and the finite alphabets actually
specified. Equal complete vectors are P2-safe; ties for scalarisation remain
support. The proofs keep those conventions distinct.

P22 scales every actual embedding row by d but does not scale the legal
completion endpoints to (d,d). The retained legal completion (1,1) is what
forces candidate and rival census. This distinction is essential and is
explicit in the proof. Both supporting thresholds invert to the embedded
hidden scalar, so the fixed monotone mapping preserves the strict inversion.
Every early raw row is independent of the hidden scalar. The comparator
therefore remains exactly D, not merely at most D. For irrational d the
scaled coordinates may be irrational; P22 correctly limits its rational-
precision claim. For d=0 there are no positive inputs and no ratio is assigned.

7. CERTIFICATE BRIDGE AND BAND LAW

The necessary crossing pair exists in two objectives because supporting
weight sets are intervals. Robust P2 supplies strict escape-coordinate
inequalities, not weak ones. Those inequalities produce the exact band
feasibility condition. The supplied construction has positive determinant
and legal observed row values, with explicit strict slack. Completing only
the candidate and its two witnesses preserves P2 and strict unsupportedness,
so the additive bridge C_d*<=C_U*<=C_d*+ceil(dN)-1 is justified.

For dN<=1, ceil(dN)-1=0. The two comparators coincide on every positive band
archive, not just on the hard family. Thus the unbounded upper and embedded
lower truly match. For shared archives across endpoint-containing bands,
the TRUE-prefix sets are the same; the global minimax ratio changes because
the admissible archive class and public policy information change.

8. FIXED-ALPHABET P22 LOWER

The five-symbol set contains both endpoints, so its TRUE-prefix sets agree
with the continuous triangle on those actual archives. The necessity proofs
use legal corner completions and strict determinant failures. Every TRUE
prefix includes the claimed common base. The two worlds remain identical
until one of the two terminal rows, even if a policy interleaves partial
tails or reads the common R rows first. Whichever terminal is read first
therefore has the same distribution in both worlds. The lower expectation
inequalities charge only compulsory rows plus a genuinely wasted tail.
Their price-balanced solution gives an exact restricted-pair game and a
global lower bound, not a global optimum.

Zero-prefix padding preserves all corners after the padding by common
positive scaling. Prefixes inside padding are excluded by extending them
monotonically to its end and using the unpadded exact prefix classification.
This supports the all-N liminf, rather than only an N=4q subsequence. The
q>=3 boundary and counterexample to a naive q=2 extension must remain.
